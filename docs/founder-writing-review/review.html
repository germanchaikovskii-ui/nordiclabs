1. Writing Style Profile

Core pattern: establish a practical problem, explain the mechanism, compare against relevant conditions, and use the evidence to reach a decision. Technical detail serves the decision rather than acting as a claim of sophistication.

The reference is German Chaykovskiy’s Comparison of Thermal Insulation Materials for Building Envelopes of Multi-storey Buildings in Saint-Petersburg, bachelor thesis, December 2010. Page references below use the PDF’s page numbers, which match its numbered pages.

Begin with a practical decision.

Thesis observation. The introduction moves from northern weather and indoor comfort to the cost and practicality of wall construction, then identifies the material-selection decision (p. 5). Page 7 translates a unit-price difference into the cost of an example building.

Website principle. Name the situation and the decision the reader faces before explaining the technology. Explain why a detail matters to that decision.

Explain the system, not an isolated feature.

Thesis observation. The introduction questions comparisons based on one or two material properties and instead considers the complete, layered wall assembly (p. 5). The computational model keeps the surrounding assembly and conditions explicit (pp. 25–27).

Website principle. Show how an input, mechanism and result relate within the existing product. Do not add capabilities to make the explanation seem more complete.

Build an argument in visible steps.

Thesis observation. The chapter introduction sets out an explicit sequence: identify the object, explain manufacture, identify requirements, and connect properties to those requirements (p. 6). The theoretical summary prepares the reader for the practical calculations (p. 23).

Website principle. Use a clear progression where the section already supports it: context → problem → mechanism → practical consequence. Avoid forcing every short website paragraph into a miniature thesis.

Make causal connections concrete.

Thesis observation. The discussion links manufacturing and material structure to behaviour, then to performance in use (pp. 7–14). Technical definitions are followed by their relevance to construction requirements (pp. 13–20).

Website principle. Prefer verbs such as connects, compares, retains, checks and informs. State what acts on what and why; use the proper technical term rather than replacing it with a vague metaphor.

Compare against explicit criteria.

Thesis observation. The worked comparison states operating conditions and wall parameters before applying the calculations (pp. 25–35). The results table places performance criteria and price together (p. 36).

Website principle. Keep the existing baseline, available data, requirements and agreed success criteria visible. Explain the basis for a decision instead of calling the technology advanced or powerful.

Reach a conclusion within the conditions.

Thesis observation. The conclusion revisits the initially inexpensive candidates after considering fire and acoustic constraints; the final selection reflects the combined criteria of the studied assembly (p. 37).

Website principle. Retain conditions and limitations. A pilot provides evidence for the next decision; it does not automatically justify expansion or guarantee a result.

Guide the reader directly without losing precision.

Thesis observation. The author addresses what the reader needs to understand before moving on, uses concrete examples and explicitly connects sections (pp. 6–7, 23). Calculation appendices provide an inspectable trail of inputs and results (pp. 40–67).

Website principle. Use active, connected sentences of varied length. Retain specialist vocabulary and necessary detail, while removing repetitive signposting and awkward phrasing.

What was not carried over: non-native grammar, repetitive transitions, awkward expressions, dated vocabulary or an academic chapter-by-chapter cadence. Some technical passages in the thesis are explicitly attributed to external sources, including the EPA manufacturing description; those passages were not treated as distinctive evidence of the founder’s own voice. The thesis is a style reference, not a source of website claims or proof of present product performance.

Two audiences, one communication philosophy

Nordic Labs: retain engineering terminology, requirements, ownership, project stages, available data and decision criteria. Explain the proposed workflow and the conditions under which it can be evaluated.

ESIVA: retain direct second-person language, daily situations and concise consumer headings. Explain what the user can do and how that action affects the remaining plan. The product pages should not read like an engineering procurement document.
