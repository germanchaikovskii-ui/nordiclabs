The foundation of our approach

Trace every requirement.
Demonstrate it with evidence.

Our approach is built around IAEA configuration-management principles: keeping the plant’s requirements, documented design and physical configuration consistent as the project develops and the plant changes.

From design onward. IAEA SSR-2/1 (Rev. 1), Requirement 3 and paragraph 3.6 address retained design knowledge, requirements management, configuration control and reviewed, documented design changes. Read SSR-2/1 ↗

Through operation and modification. IAEA SSR-2/2 (Rev. 1), Requirement 10 and paragraph 4.38 address consistency of requirements, plant records and actual configuration, with changes controlled and recorded. Read SSR-2/2, p. 15 ↗

Evidence the owner can retrieve and review.

Each link carries the source requirement, document revision, affected equipment and responsible engineer’s decision. The owner can assemble requirement-to-evidence records, identify missing links and show how a design or configuration change was assessed and approved.

This gives the owner a practical basis for implementing these principles and makes the supporting evidence easier to present during engineering reviews, audits and licensing submissions. Demonstrating compliance depends on validated project records, effective processes and authorized review.

Further guidance: IAEA Safety Reports Series No. 65, Section 2.1 — configuration-management equilibrium. The chain above illustrates our proposed implementation; it is not an IAEA-prescribed software architecture.

During construction / Contractor delivery control

From permission to start.
To accepted work.

The plans established before licensing become the owner’s day-to-day view of contract execution, work progress and acceptance.

  1. 01

    Release the work package

    Check agreed commencement-gate prerequisites, required documents and interfaces. Record the authorized person’s decision to allow work to start.

  2. 02

    Track delivery and progress

    Link contractor updates, equipment deliveries and completed quantities to contract scope, work packages and Oracle Primavera milestones.

  3. 03

    Record acceptance

    Connect claimed work quantities with inspection evidence, unresolved comments and signed acceptance records. Separate reported progress from accepted work.

  4. 04

    Connect to accounting

    Link accepted quantities and contractual milestones to accounting and payment-review workflows. Financial authorization remains with designated staff.

Gate criteria, acceptance rules and integration scope are defined from the project’s contracts and owner procedures. AI supports checking and tracking; authorized personnel make release, acceptance and payment decisions.

How the agents work together

Specialist agents.
One owner-controlled process.

Project knowledge accumulates in structured records and source documents. We configure agents to work with those records; accumulating knowledge does not require continuous retraining of an AI model.

✓

Requirements agent

Uses the project’s applicable regulations, selected standards and EPC obligations. Returns preliminary findings linked to requirement identifiers and source editions.

▤

Project knowledge agent

Classifies incoming designs, PSAR, safety analyses and supplier records. Connects information by system, equipment and revision, and flags inconsistencies across documents.

⌘

Contractor responsibility agent

Connects contract clauses with scopes, deliverables, interfaces and named contractors. Helps track commencement prerequisites, delivery milestones and the evidence needed for work acceptance.

⇄

Owner’s review workflow

Brings findings into an engineer’s review queue. Tracks assignments, comments, supplier responses and authorized approvals, preserving the decision and its supporting evidence.

A traffic-light view of preliminary findings

Each finding links back to the checked requirement, document revision and supporting evidence.

  • Green · No issue detectedNo issue found in the checks performed; engineering verification is still required.
  • Amber · Clarification neededMissing information or ambiguity needs a specialist’s assessment or supplier response.
  • Red · Potential deviationA possible conflict with the baseline is flagged for engineering investigation.

These statuses support preliminary review; they are not document acceptance, a compliance certificate or regulatory approval. Final engineering verification, acceptance and regulatory accountability remain with authorized specialists.
