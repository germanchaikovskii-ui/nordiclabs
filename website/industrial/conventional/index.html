01 / Starting point

Start with the plant you have. Or the one you plan.

We can join before design, during delivery or on an operating site. The first step is a usable baseline of your information and priorities.

  • ✓
    Requirements and records. Bring together contracts, project requirements, drawings, equipment data and existing operating documents.
  • ✓
    Information assessment. Identify source systems, missing records, conflicting revisions and the people responsible for each dataset.
  • ✓
    A focused first workflow. Choose a practical use case: supplier review, equipment information, contractor tracking or maintenance support.
  • ✓
    Agreed success criteria. Define the records, users and review measures needed to judge a demonstration before wider implementation.

02 / Engineering

Turn design information into reviewable decisions.

For a new facility or an upgrade, connect the design brief, supplier submissions and engineering decisions in one controlled environment.

  • ✓
    Requirements baseline. Structure contractual obligations, technical specifications and applicable project standards with source references.
  • ✓
    Document intake agent. Classify incoming drawings and specifications by system, equipment, supplier and revision.
  • ✓
    Engineering review. Flag gaps or inconsistencies between submissions and the agreed baseline, with references for engineers to check.
  • ✓
    Approval history. Track assignments, comments, supplier responses and authorized approvals; retain the accepted document revision.

03 / Procurement & delivery

Know what is ordered. Know what is accepted.

Connect technical requirements, equipment records and supplier commitments so procurement and project teams work from the same evidence.

  • ✓
    Equipment and material register. Link equipment tags and materials to specifications, vendor documents and approved design requirements.
  • ✓
    Supplier and contract tracking. Map deliverables, responsible suppliers, promised dates and unresolved technical comments.
  • ✓
    Delivery and acceptance. Connect receiving records and inspection evidence to ordered items and authorized acceptance decisions.
  • ✓
    Business-system connections. Link agreed procurement milestones with ERP, accounting and scheduling tools, including Oracle Primavera.

04 / Construction & handover

Keep the built facility connected to its design.

As the facility takes shape, retain the link between engineering changes, contractor work and the records the operating team will inherit.

  • ✓
    Design and change control. Review working documentation against approved design; identify affected equipment and documents when it changes.
  • ✓
    Contractor work packages. Track responsibilities, commencement prerequisites, reported quantities and signed work acceptance.
  • ✓
    Commissioning evidence. Organize test records, outstanding items and responsible sign-offs against equipment and system records.
  • ✓
    An operational handover. Deliver connected as-built drawings, manuals, asset data and approval history to the owner’s operating team.

05 / Operating production

Bring AI into the production plant you already run.

An existing facility can start here. We build on the records and systems already in use, with a focused workflow agreed with your team.

  • ✓
    Connected asset information. Bring equipment manuals, drawings, maintenance history and supplier records together around the asset.
  • ✓
    Production context. Link available production, quality and maintenance records to equipment and operating conditions.
  • ✓
    Engineering knowledge agent. Retrieve source-backed answers and assemble relevant evidence for maintenance and troubleshooting.
  • ✓
    Controlled workflows. Route findings and proposed follow-up to responsible staff; connect with existing business systems within agreed scope.

06 / Modernization

Improve the plant. Preserve what you know.

Use the accumulated engineering record to plan upgrades, assess their impact and evaluate new AI capabilities in a controlled way.

  • ✓
    Modification impact. Trace proposed changes to affected equipment, drawings, requirements and previous engineering decisions.
  • ✓
    Upgrade planning. Connect work packages, suppliers, dependencies and planned production interruptions to the project schedule.
  • ✓
    Pilot and evaluation. Test a bounded workflow against agreed criteria; use the observed result to decide what to implement next.
  • ✓
    Knowledge continuity. Keep records and approval history independent of any particular AI model, so the tools can evolve with the plant.
